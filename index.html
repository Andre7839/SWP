Methods and references

VPD is computed from air temperature and RH with the Tetens equation: es = 0.6108·exp(17.27·T/(T+237.3)) kPa, VPD = es·(1 − RH/100). Baseline SWP (MPa) = a + b·VPD (kPa); 1 MPa = 10 bar. Baselines describe the upper (least negative) limit of SWP for trees or vines with non-limiting soil water. They are linear fits, so extrapolating far beyond each study's VPD range adds uncertainty.

  • Almond, prune and other Prunus — a = −0.41, b = −0.12. McCutchan & Shackel (1992) J. Am. Soc. Hortic. Sci. 117:607–611; as cited in Shackel et al. (2021). Reproduces the UC almond/dried-plum table (60–120 °F, 10–85 % RH) within 0.05 bar. Application to peach, cherry and apricot is an extension, not a separate calibration.
  • Walnut — a = −0.278, b = −0.064. Least-squares fit to Ken Shackel's UC walnut table (UCANR); reproduces it within 0.05 bar.
  • Olive — a = −0.34, b = −0.18. Shackel et al. (2021) Front. Plant Sci. 12:791711, fitted over VPD 1.5–3.5 kPa (mid-afternoon VPD, Tetens).
  • Grapevine — a = −0.23, b = −0.068. Williams & Baeza (2007) Am. J. Enol. Vitic. 58:173–181 (fully irrigated vines, California).
  • Grapevine (Bordeaux) — a = −0.062, b = −0.0972, with the day's maximum VPD. Suter et al. (2019) Front. Plant Sci. 10:1485.
  • Pistachio — a = −0.5192, b = −0.1902. Memmi et al. (2016) Span. J. Agric. Res. 14(2):e0804 (Kerman, Spain).

Field crops such as processing tomato have no widely adopted published SWP baseline; use the Custom option with coefficients fitted to your own well-watered reference plots.
